## STAnnotator 0.2.59

Spatial transcriptomics on the tissue image, from registration and cell types to regions, niches, composition and DE
genes, cell-cell communication, trajectories and comparisons across slices, with an AI agent at your side.

**This version needs macOS 14 (Sonoma) or later on a Mac.**

### What's new

**Fixes to Refine cell types**
- A Refine round could fail to start with "cannot convert float NaN to integer" on some annotations (when the rare-type
  search had taken every cell of a cluster the marker step left untyped). It starts again.
- After an annotation of all slices together, the Refine window now opens as after one slice, and **Accept**
  annotates all slices together again with the accepted edits (before, only the current slice was annotated again).
- **Refine cell types round N** opens its window at once and sums up the annotation there; the summary takes about a
  third of the time it did (a 790,000-cell CosMx section: 26 s → 8 s, most of it reading the counts the first time).
  The marker check of the Review window is faster too (18 s → 1.4 s).
- "The agent is reviewing…" is shown only while the agent works on the current round: it no longer stays from the
  round before.

**Faster marker annotation on large samples**
- **Parallel clustering** (Type tab → Advanced; automatic, on by default): the Leiden clusterings a level tries
  (resolutions 0.5, 1, 2 and 4, and the finer ones of the rare-type search) run at once in separate processes, with
  the same clusters as one after the other. A 708,983-cell Xenium 5K lymph node: 227 s → 184 s; a 202,910-cell
  Xenium section: 20 s → 15 s. Each process needs its own memory (about 1.8 GB for 700,000 cells): only as many start
  as fit in the free memory left after what the annotation itself needs, so a computer with little free memory runs
  them one after the other as before.

**Built-in marker lists checked against single-cell atlases**
- Every marker of the built-in organ lists was checked against 23 public single-cell atlases (healthy and diseased:
  ulcerative colitis, Crohn's disease, follicular lymphoma, interstitial lung disease, lung adenocarcinoma) and the
  Human Protein Atlas: markers that a neighbouring type expresses as often moved back or went, negatives that the
  type expresses in disease went, and missing types came in (lymph node cDC1 / cDC2 / mregDC and ILC3, mesothelial
  cells, kidney connecting tubule, stomach parietal cells, and more).
- On the validation data: mouse colon MERFISH lineage agreement 0.727 / 0.887 / 0.596 / 0.468 → 0.883 / 0.892 /
  0.922 / 0.510 (four slices); Xenium 5K lymph node 0.603 → 0.610 (cell types); the human colon Xenium sections and
  the CosMx protein checks unchanged or slightly better.

**Time and memory of an annotation**
- **Neighbors** is *Approximate* by default from a million cells (all slices together count) when the time estimate
  gives it at most half the time of *Exact* on your kind of computer: the exact search grows with the square of the
  cells (2 million cells: over 4 minutes on a GPU, much longer without). Otherwise it stays *Exact*; a choice you make
  for a sample is kept for it. The agent's annotations follow the same default. It depends only on the computer's kind
  of exact search (GPU or CPU) and memory, not on the runs timed on it, so annotating again gives the same choice.
- The time estimate under Neighbors is closer to the run: it now counts the expression model on every cell and the
  rare-type search, and shows one line each for Exact, Approximate and Memory (hover for the cell count and the
  steps).
- The PCA of the marker step finds only its 30 components (Lanczos iterations) instead of reducing the whole gene
  covariance: on a 4,624-gene panel 3.4 s → 0.8 s a level, components within 1e-12, the same labels (three samples
  checked, 106,660 to 708,983 cells). PCA + UMAP gains the same.
- Annotating a sample of a large panel (Xenium 5K) needs less memory: the expression model reads the cells in smaller
  blocks (the same result), which was the peak of the annotation (708,983 cells x 4,624 genes: 8.1 GB → 6.8 GB). The
  memory estimate and the warning before a run use the new numbers.

**Also**
- **Project** (Data tab): a box of its own above the sample picks the project (or the samples in no project);
  the box below, **Slice** in a project, lists only its slices. A project opens at the slice last open in it.
  **Change** beside it (was **Projects**) sets which samples form which project and renames projects.
  The line under the sample shows only its cells, its image and whether it was filtered (the rest on hover).
  The trash beside Change removes the project with all its slices (to the data folder's trash, as Remove does one
  sample). **Overview of the project** shows the slices of the project chosen (with no project chosen, every sample as
  before). At the bottom, **Reset** is now **Reset analysis** and **Remove all** is **Remove all samples**.
- Fixed: a project added in **Change** under a name that was another project's id joined that project instead of
  making a new one; names renamed in Change showed the old ones when moving a slice then failed; removing a project
  removes nothing when one of its slices is busy (it used to remove the slices before it); Change waits while an
  annotation or analysis runs; removing the last sample starts the app afresh (the removed sample stayed open);
  **Stop refining** while a round is being prepared now stops it.
- **Refine cell types**: the proposed edits are grouped by the problem they fix (e.g. "B cells hidden under
  Ionocytes and cDC1"), with one tick box for a whole problem; a group folds away, and each edit shows its reason on
  one line (a click opens it with the evidence).
- **Refine cell types** also lists, apart from the edits, what goes against the organ's priors or your rules: the
  report shows the types whose cells lie mostly among another lineage (e.g. "Neurons among Epithelial (71%)", a sign
  of mislabeled cells) and how many cells go against each of your rules; the agent compares them with the priors, and
  you mark each finding **Real biology** (diseased tissue may break the rules; the agent does not raise it again) or
  **Mislabel** (the agent proposes an edit for it). Each finding shows a small map of the sample with its cells in red.
  Your marks carry over to later rounds. **Agent review again** is now a plain button: with edits to choose,
  **Accept** is the main one.
- **Unload H&E** (Data tab) is removed: it freed little memory (the image takes tens of MB of tiles; the app now hands
  memory back by itself). A sample unloaded before shows its image again, with its registration.
